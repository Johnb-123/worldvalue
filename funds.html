Past performance is no guide

These are the returns funds happened to earn in the last 1, 5 and 10 years. The next ten years can look very different, and a fund that topped a table often does not stay there. Within each group the funds track nearly the same shares, so the gaps between them are small.

Lower fees compound

The fee (TER) is taken every year, whether the market rises or falls. On 10,000 euro growing 7% a year for 30 years, a 0.10% fee leaves roughly 74,000 euro and a 1.00% fee roughly 57,000 euro. Small differences add up over decades.

Irish tax: exit tax and deemed disposal

Gains and income from Irish-domiciled funds, and equivalent EU/EEA/OECD funds such as these ETFs, are taxed under exit tax rather than capital gains tax. For individuals the rate is 38% from 1 January 2026 (down from 41%), per Revenue eBrief 016/26 ↗. Every 8 years you are taxed as if you had sold, even if you did not (deemed disposal); tax paid is credited when you finally sell. ETF holders usually report this themselves. Details: Revenue manuals on investment undertakings ↗ and ETFs ↗. Rules change; check Revenue.ie or a tax adviser.

How a fund differs from the stock screen

The value screen on this site looks for single companies that seem cheap on their numbers, which means picking stocks and accepting the risk that one company goes wrong. An index fund does no picking: it holds every company in an index (hundreds or thousands), so no single company matters much, and you get the market's return minus a small fee.
